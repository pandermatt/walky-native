iPhone Duo

Walky. Ready for iPhone Duo.

Unfold it and the map grows into the space. Fold it half-shut and the app takes the hint.

  • Set it down and it becomes a console

    Half folded on a table, the map stands up on the top half and the controls drop to the base under your hands — play, speed, and the switches that change what you can see.

  • Held like a book, it reads like one

    Creased the other way, the page opens with what the crowd is doing: walkies still going, how many arrived, speed, density, and how many reach a goal each second.

  • The bar goes where the system puts it

    Closed, or open in landscape, the toolbar stands up on the edge alongside every other app — because these are the system's own controls, placed by the system, not a strip of our own drawn sideways.

The engine

Calibrated, not tuned.

The numbers come from the crowd-dynamics literature, not from turning knobs until it looked right.

  • 1.34 m/s

    Weidmann's free walking speed. The scale is anchored to a real body rather than to the screen: 56 pixels to the metre.

  • Dijkstra, re-priced

    Routes come from a visibility graph rebuilt from the geometry. The routing notices the crowd every two seconds and re-costs its edges by how long people are actually taking, so they go round their own queues.

  • Speed · density · flow

    The three numbers the pedestrian literature reports, measured live while it runs — so a run can be held against published data.
